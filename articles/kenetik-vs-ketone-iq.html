What they have in common

More than either brand's marketing suggests. Both deliver drinkable ketones without requiring a ketogenic diet. Both are caffeine-free in their core products. Both raise blood BHB, which is the one effect of this category that is solidly established. Both cost real money compared to an energy drink, because the raw ingredient is expensive to make.

If your question is "do ketone drinks do anything at all," the honest category-wide answer is: they reliably raise blood ketones; the focus and endurance effects people buy them for are supported but still being studied, and they vary by person. Anyone in this category claiming certainty is selling past the science. I wrote the longer honest version in Ketones Before a Workout.

The formulation difference, in plain language

Ketone-IQ is built on R-1,3-butanediol, which your liver converts to BHB. It works. Two things follow from it, and reviewers bring both up regularly: conversion takes time, and some people report a slightly foggy, almost buzzed window while it happens.

Kenetik blends D-BHB free acid, which hits the blood directly, with R-1,3-butanediol for the longer tail. The design intent is faster onset without the woozy window and a curve instead of a spike. That is the design claim; my experience as a daily drinker is consistent with it, which you should discount appropriately given whose code is at the bottom of this page.

Taste

This category has a taste problem. Raw ketones taste like solvent, and every brand is managing that reality one way or another. Ketone-IQ's own fans describe the shot as something you get down rather than enjoy. Independent roundups have repeatedly named Kenetik the best-tasting option in the category, which matches why I can drink a can at my desk like a normal beverage instead of bracing for a shot.

Taste sounds trivial until you remember the entire value of this stuff depends on using it consistently. The version you stop drinking has an effectiveness of zero.

Certification

Kenetik is Informed Sport certified, batch-tested by an outside lab for substances banned in sport. If you are drug-tested for work or competition, that is not a nice-to-have, it is the gate, and I wrote up exactly what the certification does and does not cover in this piece. Whatever brand you consider, look the specific product up in the Informed Sport database yourself rather than trusting anything printed on a label or written by a guy with a promo code.

Where Ketone-IQ is the right answer

Writing this section is the price of being taken seriously, so here it is. If you want the largest content library and community in the category, Ketone-IQ has it. If you specifically want a caffeinated ketone shot, they make one and Kenetik does not. If you already tolerate the taste and the price and it works for you, switching costs you an adjustment for no clear gain. And their shot format travels light, though Kenetik's 2 oz shot answers that now too.

How I would actually decide

  1. If you are drug-tested, verify certification status for the exact product in the Informed Sport database and let that decide.
  2. If you drink it daily, price it per serving per month honestly and taste both once. Consistency wins, and you will only be consistent with the one you do not dread.
  3. If you are new to ketones entirely, buy the smallest quantity of either, use it for two weeks in the same slot every day, and judge on your own work, not on anyone's review. Mine included.
